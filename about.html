Constructing All-Weather Sleeves in a Higher-for-Longer Regime

Working paperWP 25-01
Published21 Jan 2025
Length11 pages
CategoryMarkets & Portfolio Strategy
Download PDF

Abstract

Traditional all-weather portfolio construction was largely developed and tested during a multi-decade period of falling and low real yields. This paper proposes an updated framework for diversified sleeves adapted to a higher-for-longer rate regime, extending beyond the conventional stock-bond-commodity split.

Key findings

  1. 01Higher starting real yields change the required allocation to floating-rate and short-duration instruments within a diversified sleeve.
  2. 02Commodity and real-asset exposure plays a larger role as an inflation hedge when bond convexity is a less reliable ballast than in prior decades.
  3. 03Volatility-targeting overlays should be recalibrated for the higher realized-volatility environment associated with elevated rate uncertainty.
  4. 04Sleeve construction should explicitly differentiate between growth shocks and inflation shocks, since the two call for materially different hedging instruments.
  5. 05Rebalancing bands may need to widen given more frequent regime transitions, in order to avoid excessive turnover and transaction costs.

Discussion

The paper backtests several sleeve constructions across historical regimes with comparably elevated real yields, and stress-tests each against both growth-shock and inflation-shock scenarios.

It concludes with practical guidance on rebalancing frequency and instrument selection for allocators seeking diversification properties similar to traditional all-weather portfolios under a structurally higher-rate regime.
