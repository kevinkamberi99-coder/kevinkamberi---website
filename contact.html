Semiconductor Capital Intensity and the Reshaping of Foundry Economics

Working paperWP 24-02
Published27 Apr 2024
Length9 pages
CategoryInvestment & Sector Research
Download PDF

Abstract

Rising capital intensity at leading-edge semiconductor process nodes is concentrating foundry economics among a narrowing set of manufacturers. This paper traces the drivers of that concentration and examines the implications for pricing power across the semiconductor value chain, from equipment suppliers to fabless designers.

Key findings

  1. 01Cost-per-transistor gains have slowed even as capital expenditure per unit of leading-edge capacity has risen, compressing the economics of successive node transitions.
  2. 02Foundry capacity at the leading edge remains concentrated among a small number of manufacturers, supporting their negotiating leverage over fabless customers on pricing and allocation.
  3. 03Government incentive programs are subsidizing new fab capacity but have had limited effect on near-term concentration at the leading edge.
  4. 04Equipment suppliers tied to extreme ultraviolet lithography and advanced packaging capture a disproportionate share of incremental industry capital expenditure.
  5. 05Capital intensity increasingly favors owners of scarce process capability over more commoditized back-end assembly and test providers.

Discussion

The analysis combines public capital expenditure disclosures, process-node cost curves, and equipment order data to map how capital intensity has evolved across the last four major node transitions.

It closes with a discussion of where in the value chain incremental capital spending is likely to translate most directly into durable pricing power, versus where returns on invested capital are more likely to compress.
