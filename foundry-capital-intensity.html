Duration as a Diversifier: Revisiting Stock–Bond Correlation Regimes

Working paperWP 23-08
Published05 Sep 2023
Length8 pages
CategoryMarkets & Portfolio Strategy
Download PDF

Abstract

The reliability of government bonds as an equity hedge depends heavily on which macro shock is dominant. This paper studies how the sign and stability of stock-bond correlation shifts across growth- and inflation-led regimes, and what that implies for sizing duration as a portfolio diversifier going forward.

Key findings

  1. 01Stock-bond correlation has historically turned positive during periods when inflation, rather than growth, is the dominant macro shock driving markets.
  2. 02The regime shift observed from 2022 reduced the effectiveness of long-duration bonds as an equity hedge relative to the prior two decades of predominantly growth-shock-driven markets.
  3. 03Realized correlation regimes tend to persist for multi-year stretches, arguing against short-horizon tactical calls on the sign of the correlation.
  4. 04Real yields and inflation volatility jointly explain more of the correlation regime than either variable alone.
  5. 05Duration allocations sized for a disinflationary, growth-shock regime should be reassessed once inflation volatility itself becomes the primary source of risk.

Discussion

The paper estimates rolling stock-bond correlation across major developed markets since 1970 and relates regime shifts to contemporaneous measures of inflation level and inflation volatility.

It proposes a simple regime classifier that allocators can use to condition the expected sign and magnitude of stock-bond correlation, and discusses implications for the duration sizing of diversified portfolios.
